Danny Barnes

Another visit for the Bad Livers' banjo man, still bending bluegrass.

Liner notes

Danny Barnes was born in Temple, Texas, and earned a degree in audio production at the University of Texas in 1985. In 1990, in Austin, he formed the Bad Livers with bassist Mark Rubin and fiddler Ralph White. He insisted they were not a bluegrass band. They played breakdowns and gospel songs, covered Motorhead and Iggy Pop, and brought in tuba, accordion and electronics.

The Bad Livers ended in 2000, and Barnes moved to Washington state, where he has worked with the jazz guitarist Bill Frisell, Dave Matthews and Chuck Leavell. He played Pickathon in 2006, 2007 and 2011.

By then he was on ATO, Dave Matthews' label, which released his solo albums Pizza Box in 2010 and Rocket in 2011.

In September 2015 he won the Steve Martin Prize for Excellence in Banjo and Bluegrass, and in 2017 he made Stove Up, a tribute to the banjo player Don Stover.
