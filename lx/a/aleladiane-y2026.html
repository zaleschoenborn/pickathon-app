Alela Diane

A Nevada City songwriter whose 2006 reissue led to Rough Trade, back for a sixth Pickathon with Who's Keeping Time? in hand.

Liner notes

She grew up in Nevada City, California, singing with her musician parents, and self-released The Pirate's Gospel in 2004. When Portland's Holocene label reissued it in 2006, The Times and NME took notice, and Rough Trade signed her. To Be Still followed in 2009; Uncut called it a quantum leap. She played Glastonbury and Primavera Sound that summer, sang on Later... with Jools Holland, and opened for Fleet Foxes in America and Europe.

Then life came into the songs. Cusp, in 2018, took its title from nearly dying in childbirth and what she understood afterward, and it carried her through a run of theaters in Britain, France and the Netherlands. In May 2026 came Who's Keeping Time?, recorded live over five days in the attic of her Victorian house, and it made Mojo's list of the year's albums. That summer she played Pickathon for the sixth time, nineteen years after the first, with three sets in two days, then toured Britain, Ireland and Europe in the fall.
