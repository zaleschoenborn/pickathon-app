Liner notes

Tom Shimura came to America from Tokyo at three and found hip-hop in the cramped studios of a college radio station. Thirty years later, he was still making records the way he started: on his own terms, with his friends.

Shimura grew up between Berkeley and Salt Lake City, the son of a Japanese father and an Italian mother, just as rap was arriving in the early 1980s. At UC Davis he found his people at KDVS: DJ Shadow, Gift of Gab, Chief Xcel and Lateef. When the labels said no, they made records anyway, on a label of their own, Solesides, and in 1993 its first release carried his song "Send Them." He was rapping as Asia Born then. Two years later he dropped the name; he wanted people to hear the rhymes before they judged where he came from.

The bet paid off slowly, then all at once. Latyrx, his duo with Lateef, made Robert Christgau's list of the best records of 1997. His solo debut, Later That Day, came out in 2003 on Quannum Projects, the label Solesides had grown into, and it made his name: Rolling Stone later counted it among the 200 greatest hip-hop albums ever made. The kid the industry had passed on became the first Asian-American solo rapper to play Lollapalooza, Coachella and Outside Lands.

After the big stages, he went back to clubs of a few hundred and to his own label, Mobile Home Recordings, and kept making records Christgau kept putting on his year-end lists: Quite a Life in 2018, Vision Board in 2022. In 2021 he lost Gift of Gab, the friend from those radio days who had been on nearly every album he made. Three years later he put out Goodbye, Sticky Rice. In the summer of 2026 he played Pickathon three times in one weekend, then spent the winter on the road with The Grouch, Eligh and Brother Ali. Thirty-five years after KDVS, still the same voice, still in rooms small enough to see his face.
